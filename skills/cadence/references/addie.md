# ADDIE outcomes and curriculum delivery

ADDIE supplies an iterative structure for analyzing, designing, developing, implementing, and evaluating instruction. Apply documented institutional requirements where relevant. Cadence's conversational checkpoints are workflow rules, not claims about institutional approval requirements. Use the interview and phase gates in SKILL.md throughout.

## Review-only interpretation

For review requests, inspect the existing course through all five phases. Do not create, revise, launch, or deliver curriculum without explicit authorization. Proposed corrections remain recommendations in the report.

| Phase | Review focus | Phase report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analysis | Intended purpose/content, learners, needs, entry knowledge, context, scope, available evidence | Scope, evidence inventory, needs/context findings and limitations |
| Design | Objectives, sequence, workload, practice and assessment alignment | Design findings and objective/activity/check map |
| Development | Existing materials' accuracy, completeness, clarity and usability | Material-specific findings with source locations and proposed corrections |
| Implementation | Delivery readiness, resources, instructor/learner guidance, accessibility and modality fit | Readiness findings; distinguish plans from observed delivery |
| Evaluation | Assessment and feedback design, available learner/outcome evidence, revision process | Supported conclusions, evidence limitations and consolidated findings |

Save reports as `deliverables/01-analysis.md` through `deliverables/05-evaluation.md`. Each contains scope and coverage, snapshot ID and citations, findings with severity and concise rationale, decisions and their status, accepted/skipped limitations, minor gaps and inaccuracies, and next-phase implications. Cite user-supplied decisions as user decisions, not as invented source passages. Maintain stable finding IDs across reports and preserve the decision history when a finding changes.

Before each phase checkpoint, verify the report's claims and run the citation checker. The conversational summary states what was established, consequential gaps resolved or accepted, minor findings, and remaining limitations; link the saved report and request approval to proceed. A gap response is not phase approval. Do not start the next phase or assign its specialists until approval arrives. Evaluation's report consolidates findings without reopening settled decisions.

For builds and authorized revisions, use the outcomes below with the same five phase reports and approval checkpoints. Keep evidence records to sources consulted, checks performed, results, concise rationales, recommendations and user decisions; do not log private internal thinking.

## Analysis

Apply [artifact-deliverables.md](artifact-deliverables.md): save the catalog's consolidated **Analysis Summary Report**, including its needs assessment, JTA, doctrinal synthesis, and source sufficiency sections. The shorter records below can support that report; they do not replace it for a catalog-governed build.

Establish the course's purpose, learners, entry knowledge, intended performance, delivery conditions, and available resources. Review existing materials and the applicable institutional process. Separate demonstrated needs from assumptions. Where a performance problem motivates the work, consider whether access, process, incentives, workload, or resources explain it before treating instruction as the solution.

**Save:** a concise course or lesson brief, a source inventory, and the few unresolved assumptions that affect design. For a review, state which material was inspected and what the requested evaluation can establish.

## Design

Save the catalog's consolidated **Course Design Document**, with all six numbered sections and traceability back to the Analysis Summary Report. Maintain its subsection completion markers as each section is written and checked.

Translate the agreed purpose into observable objectives, a sequence of lessons, meaningful practice, and proportionate evidence of learning. Fit workload and activities to the known learners and delivery setting. Link each objective to the lesson, activity, and learning check that supports it. Use institutional templates and required review stages when the retrieved evidence establishes their applicability.

**Save:** a course map or lesson design, proposed schedule, and objective/activity/check traceability. For a narrow revision, update only the affected parts while noting consequential dependencies. Do not infer an assessment problem merely because an assessment was not included in an initial excerpt.

## Development

Use the catalog's 22 artifact types across five categories as the artifact plan, following [artifact-deliverables.md](artifact-deliverables.md). Produce the applicable documents, retain explained exclusions, and keep a separate completion marker for every type. The general table below supplements that catalog; it does not replace its named outputs.

Create usable materials from the design and cited subject sources. A build request is unfinished if it contains only headings, placeholders, a plan to write, or recommendations for someone else to create the instructional content.

For a course build, include the following where they apply to the agreed scope:

| Artifact | Usable content |
| --- | --- |
| Course guide or syllabus | Purpose, learner prerequisites, objectives, lesson sequence, schedule, materials, and applicable local course policies |
| Lesson plans | Objectives, realistic timings, preparation, instructor actions, learner tasks, materials, and learning checks |
| Learner materials | Explanations, examples, readings or precise source references, practice prompts, and any needed handouts |
| Activities and assignments | Complete instructions, conditions, expected outputs, time allowance, and facilitation or feedback notes |
| Assessments, when needed | Actual questions or performance tasks plus separate answers, scoring guidance, or rubrics tied to objectives |
| Instructor guide | Delivery preparation, facilitation notes, transitions, likely misconceptions, and appropriate adaptations |

A single-lesson request may combine these in one coherent document. A supplied template can combine or rename artifacts. Slide decks are optional outputs when requested; a slide outline does not stand in for a usable course or for an actual requested presentation file.

Maintain a short distinction between sourced facts, design choices, and unresolved assumptions. Cite factual content at a useful level without cluttering every instructor action. Record applicable institutional requirements and the artifact sections that satisfy them. When evidence does not support a needed subject fact, retrieve it or mark the specific uncertainty; do not fill the gap with an invented authoritative claim.

## Implementation preparation

Prepare the instructor and learner experience: materials, resources, setup, accessibility accommodations supported by the available information, run-of-show, timing, and contingency notes. Check that learners can actually carry out each activity with the supplied information and resources.

**Save:** a practical delivery checklist and instructor guidance. State **prepared for delivery** when this is what occurred. Generating documents does not show that teaching, an LMS launch, instructor training, or learner participation happened. Report actual implementation only when the user supplies or the workflow produces verifiable implementation evidence.

## Evaluation

Conduct formative review of what can be inspected: subject correctness, objective alignment, workload, clarity, institutional requirements, completeness, and practical usability. Base conclusions on the available artifacts and the actual coverage achieved. For evaluation beyond artifact review, define what evidence to collect, when to collect it, and how it will inform revision.

**Save:** a brief formative findings/changes record and an evaluation plan with measures, collection points, and decision criteria. Proposed thresholds are design choices unless a cited requirement establishes them. Do not report learner effectiveness, transfer, completion, satisfaction, or institutional approval without the corresponding evidence.

## Handover check

- Requested curriculum files contain actual usable content and can be opened.
- Objectives connect to relevant lessons, activities, and learning checks.
- Applicable institutional requirements are cited and mapped to the delivered materials; unresolved conflicts are explicit.
- Subject sources have valid passage citations and a source snapshot identifies the versions used.
- The coverage record states what was inspected and what was skipped or unreadable.
- The delivery checklist and evaluation plan fit the known setting.
- Completion language distinguishes artifact preparation, formative inspection, actual delivery, and measured outcomes.

Keep supporting records short enough to use. A compact course map may serve as the traceability map; a single source/decision record may hold provenance, coverage, assumptions, and remaining issues. Do not create process documents that add no value to the requested curriculum.
